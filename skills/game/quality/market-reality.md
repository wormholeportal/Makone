# Market Reality

## Current Distillation

Steam is crowded enough that "technically works" is invisible. A concept needs
both a familiar demand bucket and a sharp first-frame twist.

Research signals to internalize:

- Newzoo projected the global 2025 games market around $189B, with PC/console
  growth tied to blockbuster releases, premium pricing, and existing-player
  monetization. That means a small web game must win by clarity and novelty,
  not by content volume.
- Chris Zukowski's Steam analysis reported 20,282 new Steam releases in 2025
  and 608 reaching 1,000 reviews. The useful lesson is not "ship to Steam"; it
  is that discoverability is brutal, and rough-but-fun games can still break
  through if the hook is immediate.
- The hot small-team patterns in 2025-2026 are tight-scope, high-legibility
  loops: survivors-likes, co-op panic games, horror, rage/clip games,
  autobattlers, idle/desktop games, and strange physics toys.
- Trend chasing without a real player fantasy creates slop. A trend is only
  useful when the target player already wants that loop and your twist is
  visible in one screenshot.

## Makone Market Filter

Before design, answer:

| Gate | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Demand bucket | The game fits a known appetite: mastery, chaos, cozy creation, horror panic, power fantasy, puzzle aha, collection, score chase. |
| Screenshot hook | One still frame explains the fantasy and contains one impossible-to-ignore object/action. |
| Five-word pitch | A stranger understands and reacts without genre jargon. |
| Streamable moment | A 10-second clip has either spectacle, panic, failure comedy, mastery, or surprise. |
| Scope truth | The smallest version can be made in one scene and still show the hook. |

If two gates fail, do not code. Pick a sharper fantasy.

## Recommended Bets For Makone

Default toward loops where Three.js creates visible value fast:

- **Survivors-like spectacle**: one arena, many enemies, huge feedback stack.
- **Physics comedy / panic**: unstable objects, near-misses, visible failure.
- **Shmup / score chase**: pure readability + patterns + neon.
- **Endless runner / rage runner**: instant retry, clip-worthy mistakes.
- **Tower-defense toybox**: clear path, satisfying destruction, upgrade choices.
- **Puzzle with visual transformation**: lasers, mirrors, gravity, color grammar.

Treat these as dangerous for Makone first-pass work:

- Full co-op networking.
- Large deckbuilder/card pools without a simulator.
- Open-world survival craft.
- Visual novel at real content scale.
- Match-3 as a production economy.

## Anti-Trend Rule

Do not write "like X but with Y" unless Y changes one of:

- the core verb,
- the risk/reward structure,
- the visual read,
- the social/clip moment,
- the build-crafting decision.

If Y is only theme, the game is a skin.
